# AI Critique

I used OpenAI Codex for two main purposes in this assignment: creating a completion plan and reviewing the heuristic mapping of my keyboard-navigation checklist item. The planning response was useful because it converted a large specification into an ordered workflow covering checklist execution, evidence capture, compatibility testing, defect reporting, reconciliation, and validation. It helped me avoid missing required deliverables. However, the plan was still generic. It could not access my authenticated EMS session or know whether a particular control, loading state, or error message actually appeared.

The second interaction evaluated GUI-051, which I had thought of and written myself. Codex confirmed that keyboard navigation can reasonably map to Nielsen's Flexibility and efficiency of use because it provides an alternative interaction method and can improve efficiency for keyboard users. Its most valuable criticism was that the phrase "without being trapped" also concerns user control and accessibility. It also noted that GUI-051 combines focus order, Enter activation, and absence of a keyboard trap, so the item is not perfectly atomic. This explanation improved my understanding without changing the ownership of the idea.

The main risk of using AI in software testing is that polished wording can look like evidence. Codex could describe plausible bugs, severities, and expected results even when it had not observed the application. I therefore treated AI output as planning and review support only. I personally executed the tests, judged Passed, Failed, or N/A, captured screenshots, and recorded real submission timestamps. In future work, I will ask AI to state its assumptions, compare alternative heuristic mappings, and clearly separate suggestions from verified observations.
